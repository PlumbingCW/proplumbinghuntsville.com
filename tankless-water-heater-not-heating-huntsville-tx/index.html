Tankless Water Heater Not Heating in Huntsville, TX: Causes

A tankless water heater that stops heating usually has a gas or power problem, a clogged filter, scale or a flow issue. A few checks are safe for a homeowner. The rest need a plumber.

Start with the display. Most units show an error code that points toward the fault, and the manual lists what each code means.

Written by the Huntsville Pro Plumbing team · Published November 13, 2026.

Simple checks first

Confirm the unit has power and that the display is on. Make sure the gas valve to the unit is open and other gas appliances work. Check that the temperature is set correctly and that a hot tap is fully open. Some units need a minimum flow to fire, so a barely open tap may not trigger heating.

If the display shows an error code, write it down. A plumber can often tell what is wrong from the code before arriving.

  • Power or breaker is on.
  • Gas valve is open and other gas appliances work.
  • Temperature setting is correct.
  • The tap is open enough to trigger the unit.
  • Note the error code on the display.

Scale is the quiet culprit

Minerals deposit on the heat exchanger and narrow its passages. The unit then heats less water, reaches its limit and fails to hit the set temperature. A descaling flush with a pump and a mild solution usually restores output.

The City of Huntsville's report gives no hardness figure, so test your own tap. If your water is hard, flushing yearly matters more. See the tankless water heater page for service details.

Gas, vent and flame problems

A tankless unit needs enough gas at the right pressure. A gas line that is too small may let the unit ignite at low demand and fail at high demand. Blocked vents trip safety switches that shut the unit off. Never remove a panel or bypass a safety device.

If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or the gas utility before you call a plumber.

What the error codes usually mean

Codes vary by maker, but they tend to fall into a few groups. Ignition codes point to gas supply or the flame sensor. Vent codes point to a blocked or disconnected exhaust. Temperature codes point to scale or a failing sensor. Flow codes point to a clogged inlet filter or a low water supply.

Your manual lists the exact meaning. Write down the code and when it appears, since that helps a plumber arrive with the right parts.

When to call a plumber

Call if the code repeats after a reset, if you smell gas, if the unit leaks or if flushing does not help. A plumber can test the sensors, inspect the venting and measure gas pressure. Sometimes the best answer is a new heat exchanger or a new unit, which our water heater replacement page explains.

Do not try these

  • Do not remove the front cover while the unit has power.
  • Do not bypass a safety switch or vent sensor.
  • Do not use a tankless unit with a damaged vent pipe.
  • Do not keep resetting a unit that trips repeatedly.
  • Do not ignore a gas smell.

Avoiding the next failure

Flush the unit on the manufacturer's schedule, clean the inlet filter and keep the area around the unit clear. Install isolation valves if they are missing, since they make flushing easy. Schedule service before winter if the unit is exposed.

A local note on water quality

The City of Huntsville reports chloramine averaging 3.09 mg/L, ranging from 1.12 to 3.92 mg/L through the year. Chloramine can wear rubber seals over time, so ask the plumber to check inlet filter screens and rubber parts when the unit is serviced.

Vent and gas connections on a tankless unit are governed by the International Fuel Gas Code and the International Plumbing Code (IPC), both listed by the City of Huntsville.
