How To Detect a Water Leak in Your Home in Huntsville, TX

Most home water leaks show up on the bill before they show up on the floor. A meter test takes ten minutes and tells you if water is moving when it should not be.

This guide covers the meter test, the toilet dye test, common signs and when to bring in leak detection equipment.

Written by the Huntsville Pro Plumbing team · Published November 13, 2026.

The meter test

A moving meter with everything off is the strongest sign of a leak. To narrow it, close the main shutoff at the house. If the meter keeps moving, the leak is between the meter and the house. If it stops, the leak is inside.

  1. Turn off every fixture and appliance that uses water, including the ice maker and the irrigation timer.
  2. Locate the water meter and read the dial or the small leak indicator.
  3. Wait fifteen to thirty minutes without using water, then look again.
  4. If the dial or indicator has moved, water is escaping somewhere between the meter and the fixtures.

The toilet dye test

Toilets are the most common hidden leak. A worn flapper lets water seep from the tank to the bowl, so the toilet refills quietly all day. Put a few drops of food coloring in the tank and wait twenty minutes without flushing. If color appears in the bowl, the flapper is leaking.

Chloraminated water tends to wear rubber parts faster. The City of Huntsville's report lists chloramine, so flappers are a first wear point. See toilet repair for the fix.

Signs of a hidden leak

Outdoor leaks show up as soggy yard patches, unusually green grass or a wet area near the meter. Irrigation lines are a common source.

  • Water bill that rises without a change in use.
  • Sound of running water when nothing is on.
  • Warm spots on a slab floor.
  • Musty smell or mold in a closet or cabinet.
  • Stained ceilings or soft floors.
  • Low water pressure.

Reading your water bill like a detective

Compare the last several bills. A leak shows up as a step up that stays high, while a seasonal rise from watering the lawn comes and goes. If the usage figure jumped but your habits did not, start with the meter test.

Write down the meter reading each evening and morning for a few days. A change overnight, with no one using water, means water is escaping.

When equipment is needed

Once you know there is a leak but not where, a plumber uses acoustic listening, thermal imaging and pressure testing to find it without opening walls. That approach limits damage and cost. Our leak detection page describes the process, and slab leak repair covers the fix if the leak is under the floor.

Leaks in New Waverly and Riverside are treated the same way, though a well-fed property may have a pump or tank to check.

Outdoor and irrigation leaks

Outdoor lines are easy to damage with digging, and they leak for weeks before anyone notices.

  • Wet patches in the yard that do not dry.
  • A sprinkler zone that stays soggy after it runs.
  • Water bubbling near the meter box.
  • Low pressure at an outdoor spigot.
  • Unusually green strips over the line path.

What to do while you wait

If you suspect a leak, close the main shutoff when you are away and photograph any water damage for insurance. Keep the bill that shows the jump, since it documents the timing. If the leak is large, call the 24/7 line.

A local note on utilities and bills

If you receive a utility bill from the City of Huntsville, a high bill may be worth asking the utility about, since a documented leak can matter for an adjustment. Ask the utility what documentation it wants. We do not know what policy applies to your account, so ask them directly.

Pressure tests that plumbers run on supply lines are modeled on requirements in the International Plumbing Code (IPC), which the City of Huntsville lists at its 2024 edition.
