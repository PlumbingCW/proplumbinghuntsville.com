Sewer Inspection in Huntsville, TX

A sewer camera inspection lets you see inside a pipe you cannot reach, and it is the cheapest way to avoid guessing. In Huntsville a plumber feeds a small camera through a cleanout and records what it finds, from roots and cracks to bellies and collapsed sections.

Sewer inspection is a recorded video look inside your sewer line, with the location and depth of any defect marked on the surface. It is used before buying a house, after repeat backups and before a repair, so the repair is sized to the real damage.

When to schedule a camera inspection

Book one before you buy an older house, after a second or third backup in a year and before any excavation. A camera look can also confirm that a recent repair or cleaning worked. Landlords use it to document a line before a new tenant moves in.

If a sewage backup is happening now, call the 24/7 line instead and ask for sewer line cleaning first. A camera cannot see through a full line.

  • Buying or selling a house.
  • Repeat backups or slow drains.
  • Before trenchless lining or spot repair.
  • After root removal to check the pipe.
  • A damp yard patch or sewer smell outdoors.

What the camera shows

  1. The plumber locates the cleanout and feeds a camera on a flexible cable into the line.
  2. Pipe material, joints, roots, cracks, offsets and bellies are recorded.
  3. A locator at the surface marks the depth and position of any defect.
  4. You receive the recording and a plain explanation of what it means.

Pipe materials and what to expect in older homes

Older homes may have clay tile, cast iron or fiber pipe, and newer ones use PVC. Each fails differently. Clay joints admit roots, cast iron scales and corrodes and fiber pipe can deform. The Texas State Historical Association describes Walker County soils as sands and clays, and clay soil movement can offset joints in any older pipe.

If the camera finds a problem, the next step is a repair quote, covered under sewer line repair. A clear line costs nothing further.

Reading the report

A good report names the pipe material, marks each defect on the video and states the distance from the cleanout. Ask for the recording as a file, not just a summary, and keep it. If you are buying a house, the recording is a useful bargaining tool, and a seller may agree to a repair credit when it shows a real defect.

A report that finds nothing is also worth keeping. It shows the condition of the line on a given date, which helps if a problem shows up later.

Septic versus city sewer

Some Huntsville properties are on city sewer and others use septic systems. A camera inspection of a septic drain line is similar, but the tank has its own checks. Tell the dispatcher which one you have so the right equipment arrives. For property buyers outside the city, ask the seller what the tank type and last service date are.

Before the plumber arrives

  • Clear access to the cleanout.
  • Tell the plumber about prior backups.
  • Avoid running water heavily just before the visit.
  • Share any earlier repair records.
  • Mention if the home has a septic system.
