About downtown Huntsville

A local real estate guide calls Sam Houston Avenue the spine of Huntsville's old downtown, with a central square and courthouse. The Texas Commission on the Arts designated the Huntsville Cultural District in September 2009, according to a Wikipedia summary. The City's Cultural District tour guide describes buildings from the 1840s into the 1940s.

One example is a theatre that opened as a movie house in 1947 and reopened as a performing arts venue in 2002. Mixed ages and uses mean no single plumbing answer. A century-old storefront and a recent renovation can share a block, so we look at the specific building and not the district.

Permits, leases and historic buildings

A new plumbing system or the replacement of water or waste lines in a Huntsville building needs a City permit. Commercial work can carry additional requirements, so confirm them with the City permit office before work begins. We did not find plumbing rules specific to the Cultural District, so ask the City before you open walls in a historic building.

In a leased storefront, the lease usually decides who handles repairs. Check it before you call, and tell the plumber who the property manager is.

If water is spreading in the shop
