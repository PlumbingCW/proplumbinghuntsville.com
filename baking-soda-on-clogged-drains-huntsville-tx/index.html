Does Baking Soda Unclog a Drain? A Huntsville, TX Guide

Baking soda and vinegar can freshen a drain and loosen a light film, but they rarely clear a real clog. If water is standing in the sink or tub, the blockage is usually hair, grease or a solid object that fizzing will not move.

Here is what the mix does, what to try first and where the limits are.

Written by the Huntsville Pro Plumbing team · Published November 13, 2026.

What baking soda and vinegar actually do

Baking soda is a mild base and vinegar is a mild acid. Together they fizz, which can loosen soft residue on pipe walls. That is a cleaning effect, not a clearing one. A hair clog or a plug of hardened grease sits in the pipe and needs to be pulled out or broken up.

The mix works best as monthly upkeep on a drain that still flows. It does little for a drain that is already slow or blocked.

  • Good for: odors and light film in a flowing drain.
  • Limited on: slow drains with hair or soap scum.
  • Poor on: full blockages and grease plugs.
  • No effect on: roots or a broken pipe.

A method that is worth trying

Hot water is fine. Boiling water can soften PVC joints and crack a porcelain bowl, so use very hot tap water on those.

  1. Remove standing water if you can, and take out the stopper or strainer.
  2. Pour in half a cup of baking soda, then half a cup of white vinegar, and cover the opening for about fifteen minutes.
  3. Flush with a kettle of hot, not boiling, water for plastic pipes.
  4. Test the flow. If it is still slow, move to a plunger or a drain snake.

Chemical drain cleaners: a caution

Do not follow baking soda with a store-bought chemical cleaner, and never mix cleaners. Some combinations release harmful gases. Leftover chemicals can also burn skin when a plumber later opens the trap. If you already used a chemical product, tell the plumber when you call.

Repeated chemical use can also damage older metal pipes. If a drain keeps clogging, the line may need a camera look, which our sewer inspection page describes.

Why the fizz feels like it works

The bubbling is satisfying to watch, and bubbles carry a little cleaning action up the pipe wall. That is why many people report a better drain after using it. The improvement is usually temporary, because the material that caused the slowdown is still in the line and begins collecting again.

A real clearing job removes the buildup. A hand tool or cable pulls hair out, and water pressure scours grease off the wall. Fizzing alone does neither, which is why a clog that returns within days needs a different approach.

When the clog is not in the sink

If several drains gurgle, a toilet bubbles when the shower runs or water backs up in the lowest drain in the house, the blockage is probably in the main line. No amount of baking soda will fix that. Stop using water and call for drain cleaning.

A quick decision guide

  • Water drains but smells: baking soda, vinegar and a hot flush are a fair try.
  • Water drains slowly: use a hair tool or plunger before anything else.
  • Water stands in the sink or tub: stop and use a plunger or snake.
  • Two or more fixtures affected: stop using water and call a plumber.
  • Clog returns within a week: ask about cable cleaning or jetting.

Keeping drains clear

Use hair catchers in showers, wipe greasy pans before washing and do not pour fat down the sink. Run hot water after dishes. If a bathroom sink slows every few months, hair and soap scum are building on the pipe wall, and a cleaning is overdue.

A local note on roots and soil

The Texas State Historical Association describes Walker County soils as sands and clays, and pine and oak roots in this forest country search out leaking joints. A drain that clogs repeatedly near the street side of a house is often a root problem and not a grease problem, and no home remedy will touch it.

Traps and vents are required by the International Plumbing Code (IPC), the plumbing code the City of Huntsville lists at its 2024 edition, which is why a vent problem can slow a drain.
