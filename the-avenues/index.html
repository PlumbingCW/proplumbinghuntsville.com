Plumber in The Avenues, Huntsville, TX

The Avenues is a local name for the lettered avenue streets in the older part of Huntsville, near downtown and Sam Houston State University. Listings show homes on Avenue J, Avenue L, Avenue M, Avenue O, Avenue P, Avenue Q and Avenue T.

We found no official boundary for the Avenues, so this page describes the area by its street names and does not draw one. If your address is on a lettered avenue, call and describe what is happening.

What the Avenues are, and what is not documented

A local rental listing places a townhome development in the heart of the historic Avenues of Huntsville. A local real estate guide says restorations of historic and established homes are common in Huntsville's core. We did not find a city or neighborhood-association source that sets formal boundaries, so we treat the name as a local label and not an official district.

Two houses on these streets are on the National Register of Historic Places. The John W. Thomason House at 1207 Avenue J was listed in 1982, and Sam Houston's Woodland home on Avenue L was listed in 1974. They show the area has real history. They do not tell you what is inside the walls of the house next door.

Older houses call for facts, not guesses

An established house may have its original plumbing, a mix of old and new pipe, or a full replacement from a past remodel. Age alone cannot tell you which. A plumber who checks the shutoff valves, looks at the pipe under sinks and at the water heater, and runs a camera down the sewer line gives you facts that a listing year cannot.

That is the angle for this page. We do not claim that homes on the lettered avenues have any particular pipe material or failure. We suggest a short, specific list of things to have checked.

  • Shutoff valves that turn freely and seal.
  • Supply pipe type visible at the water heater and under sinks.
  • Water pressure at an outdoor hose bib.
  • Sewer line condition by camera before a purchase.
  • Any wet staining on ceilings, cabinets or floors.

Permits and approvals for older properties

Replacing water or waste piping, adding plumbing and remodeling a bathroom with a shower all fall under City of Huntsville permit rules. The International Plumbing Code (IPC) 2024 is among the codes the City applies, and a licensed plumber normally handles the paperwork. If your project touches a historic house, ask the City which approvals apply before work starts. We did not find a published plumbing rule specific to the Avenues, so a direct question to the City is the safest route.

Before you call about a leak

  • Turn the main water shutoff to closed when water keeps spreading.
  • Cut power to wet rooms at the breaker.
  • Photograph the damage and note the time.
  • Stop running any drain that is backing up.
  • Keep your street address handy for the call.
