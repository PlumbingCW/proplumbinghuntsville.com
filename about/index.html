About Huntsville Pro Plumbing

Huntsville Pro Plumbing is a 24/7 plumbing dispatch line for Huntsville, Texas and nearby Walker County towns. This page explains how a call is handled and what you should check about any plumber before work starts.

Call (855) 575-2890

How a call is handled

  1. You call the 24/7 line and describe the problem. The dispatcher asks where the water is, what has been shut off and whether anyone is at risk.
  2. A licensed and insured plumber is sent. You are told what to do while you wait, such as closing the main valve or switching off breakers.
  3. On site the plumber diagnoses the cause and explains the repair and price before work begins.
  4. The job ends with a test of the repair, and you receive an itemized invoice.

Checking any plumber's license in Texas

The Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners tells consumers to get the name of the company's Responsible Master Plumber and look up that license number in its public license search. Master Plumber numbers begin with the letter M, and the number should appear on advertisements, written estimates and invoices.

The board does not regulate plumbers' prices, so it also recommends getting estimates and warranties in writing. You can do all of this at tsbpe.texas.gov before you agree to any work, whoever the plumber is.

What this site does not do

We do not publish prices as flat numbers because plumbing jobs vary. We do not respond to gas leaks, so if you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first. We do not do water damage restoration, so ask your insurer for a drying company after a flood.

We also do not make health claims about drinking water. For questions about what is in your tap water, the City of Huntsville publishes an annual drinking water quality report, and your physician is the right person for health questions.

What a plumbing call looks like at night

Most emergencies do not happen at noon. A water heater tank gives way at two in the morning, a toilet overflows during a holiday dinner, a drain backs up on a Sunday. The line is answered during those hours, because a dispatcher who is asleep cannot send anyone.

When you call, the dispatcher asks four things: where the water is coming from, whether it is reaching a floor, whether you have found the main shutoff and what your street address is. Those answers decide which plumber is sent and what truck he loads.

While the plumber travels, the dispatcher stays practical. You may be asked to close the main, switch off a breaker, move valuables or keep children away from standing water. These steps limit damage before anyone arrives.

How to compare plumbing quotes

Ask each plumber to put the scope in writing. A fair quote names the work, the parts, the labor and any permit. It tells you what is not included and what could change the price if something hidden is found. A quote that is a single number with no description is hard to compare.

Ask who will do the work and whether that person holds a license. Ask what warranty applies to labor and to parts. And ask how the plumber handles surprises, since an old house often has one.

Insurance and your plumbing

Homeowner policies often cover sudden and accidental water damage, such as a pipe that bursts, and often exclude wear, slow leaks and neglect. Policies differ, so read yours or ask your agent before an emergency. Photograph damage before cleanup and keep every receipt.

We do not give insurance advice and we do not file claims for you. We can describe in writing what failed and what was repaired, which is what most adjusters ask for.

How the local pages are written

Each page on this site uses facts from public local sources, such as the City of Huntsville's annual water quality report, the City's permit information, the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners and the Texas State Historical Association. Where a number is not published, such as water hardness, the page says so and tells you how to test your own tap.

The guides on the plumbing blog follow the same rule. Each ends with a local note that names the type of source it draws from, so you can check it.
