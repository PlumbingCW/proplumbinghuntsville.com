Emergency Garbage Disposal Repair in Huntsville, TX

A disposal that stops and floods the sink can ruin a kitchen night. Switch it off, leave the water off and call our 24/7 line in Huntsville. Never put a hand into the disposal, even when it is off.

Garbage disposal repair clears jams, replaces leaking seals and fixes electrical faults, and replaces the unit when the motor or housing has failed. It also covers the drain and dishwasher connection that the disposal shares.

Disposal jammed, humming or leaking

A humming disposal that will not spin is jammed. A silent disposal may have tripped its reset button or lost power. A leak from the bottom of the unit means the housing has failed, and from the top or side usually points to a seal or drain connection.

Turn off power at the breaker before touching it. A plumber uses a hex wrench at the bottom to free the flywheel and tests the reset. If the unit leaks from the body, replacement is the only fix.

  • Humming, not spinning: jammed flywheel.
  • Silent: reset button or power.
  • Leak from the bottom: housing failure.
  • Leak at the top: sink flange seal.
  • Standing water: clogged drain.

What to do and not do with a jammed disposal

  • Switch off power at the wall switch and the breaker.
  • Do not put your hand in the grinding chamber.
  • Use tongs to remove anything you can see.
  • Press the reset button only after the unit cools.
  • Do not pour drain cleaner into it.

Fixing the clog behind the disposal

Standing water in both sink basins usually means the blockage is beyond the disposal. A disposal grinds food into small pieces that stick to the grease coating in the line. See drain cleaning for clearing the line.

If the dishwasher drain backs into the sink, the connection at the disposal inlet may be clogged. The plumber knocks out the plug on a new unit and checks the air gap or high loop.

What not to put down a disposal

Running cold water before, during and for a few seconds after use helps carry waste past the trap.

  • Grease, fat and oil.
  • Coffee grounds in large amounts.
  • Fibrous vegetables such as celery and corn husks.
  • Pasta and rice that swell in water.
  • Bones, fruit pits and hard objects.
  • Chemical drain cleaners.

Installing a new disposal

  1. Power is shut off and the old unit is unmounted and removed.
  2. The sink flange is cleaned and resealed, and the mounting ring is set.
  3. The new disposal is attached, wired or plugged in, and connected to the drain and dishwasher line.
  4. The unit is tested with water and the plumber checks for leaks.

Habits that keep a disposal alive

Run cold water while it works and for several seconds after. Feed small amounts. Avoid fibrous peels, bones, grease and large pits. Citrus does not harm it but it does not clean it either. Do not pour grease down, because it solidifies downstream and creates the clogs that make a disposal back up.

If you rent in Huntsville, report a jam early. A disposal run in a jam burns the motor.

Reset and jam checks you can do safely

With power off at the switch and the breaker, use a hex key in the bottom of the unit to turn the flywheel back and forth and free a jam. Press the red reset button on the bottom after it cools. Never put a hand into the grinding chamber, even with the power off, and use tongs for anything you can see.
