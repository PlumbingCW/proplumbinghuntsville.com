Crawl Space and Under-House Water Leaks in Huntsville, TX

Water under the house can come from a plumbing leak or from groundwater and runoff. The fix is different, so the first job is to find out which one you have.

This guide covers the signs, simple checks and when to call for leak detection or repair.

Written by the Huntsville Pro Plumbing team · Published November 13, 2026.

Plumbing leak or groundwater?

A plumbing leak is steady and often follows the pipe. It drips from a joint or sprays from a split, and the water meter moves even when nothing is on. Groundwater tends to show up after heavy rain, spreads across the floor and does not move the meter. East Texas gets roughly 46 inches of rain a year, so rain-driven moisture is common.

Close every fixture and watch the water meter for fifteen minutes, or have a plumber confirm it with leak detection.

What to look for under the house

Wear protective gear and use a flashlight. Do not enter a crawl space if you see standing water near electrical equipment.

  • Drips or wet insulation near pipes.
  • Rust on supply lines or drain pipes.
  • Damp soil directly under a bathroom or kitchen.
  • Musty smell or visible mold on framing.
  • Standing water along one wall.
  • Gaps in the vapor barrier.

Why crawl spaces leak

Older supply lines corrode, drain pipes crack and fittings loosen as soil shifts. A leaking water heater, a failed washing machine hose or a drain that has pulled loose from a toilet can all drip below the floor. The Texas State Historical Association describes Walker County soils as sands and clays, and clay moves with moisture, which stresses pipes.

Repeated leaks in different places can mean the pipe itself is failing, covered on the repiping page.

What a plumber does

  1. Pressure tests the supply lines and checks the meter.
  2. Inspects the supply and drain lines visually and with a camera if needed.
  3. Uses listening gear or a thermal camera to find a hidden leak.
  4. Repairs the section or recommends a reroute, then dries and documents the area.

Safety before you go under the house

Crawl spaces hold hazards that are easy to miss. Wires hang low, insulation drops, nails stick out of framing and animals nest in the dark. Wear gloves, a mask and knee pads, bring a bright light and tell someone you are going in.

If you see standing water near electrical equipment, stay out and call. If a drain pipe is leaking, avoid contact with the water, since it may contain sewage.

Drainage, grading and vapor barriers

If the plumbing is sound, the answer is outside the pipes. Soil sloping toward the house, clogged gutters or downspouts that dump at the foundation push water under it. A vapor barrier on the ground and sealed vents reduce humidity. A drainage contractor, not a plumber, handles regrading and French drains.

A plumber can still help by checking whether a sump pump or an irrigation line is adding to the problem.

How long wet framing can wait

Wood that stays wet for days begins to grow mold, and soft spots can appear over longer periods. Stop the source first, then dry the area with air movement and, where needed, a dehumidifier. A restoration company handles drying and mold testing, which is outside the scope of plumbing.

Photograph the damage before you dry anything out, because an insurer may ask for it.

Where this matters most

Older raised foundation homes in Huntsville, plus rural homes around New Waverly and Riverside, are the ones most likely to have exposed crawl space plumbing. See leak detection for how a plumber finds the source.

A local note on rain and soil

The Texas State Historical Association reports that Walker County averages roughly 46 inches of rain a year and has sandy and clay soils. Heavy rain events can saturate the ground around a raised foundation within hours, so check the crawl space after a big storm.

Pipe supports and protection under a house are covered by the International Plumbing Code (IPC), which the City of Huntsville lists at its 2024 edition.
