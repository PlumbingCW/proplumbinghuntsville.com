Plumber Near Sam Houston State University, Huntsville, TX

Sam Houston State University sits in central Huntsville, and the rental streets near it have a different plumbing rhythm from a family neighborhood. Leases end, tenants change and small leaks go unreported.

This page is for landlords, property managers and tenants. We describe the area by landmarks and street names because we found no official boundary.

About the area around campus

Sam Houston Normal Institute was established in 1879, and the university is a central part of Huntsville. A neighborhood data site says most residential real estate in the campus tract is renter occupied. A student housing guide lists apartments, condos, townhomes and rental houses on streets such as Sendero Drive, Normal Park Drive, Badger Lane, Avenue M and Avenue N.

Treat those as supporting sources and not official records. Avenue M and Avenue N also belong to the Avenues, which shows how the two areas overlap.

Landlord and tenant plumbing

Texas landlord and tenant duties are set out in the Texas Property Code and in each lease, so read yours before deciding who calls and who pays. We cannot give legal advice on that question.

As a general rule, tenants should report leaks, slow drains and missing hot water in writing as soon as they notice them. Small problems grow, and in a multi-unit building water damage can reach neighbors.

  • Keep grease, wipes and food scraps out of drains and disposals.
  • Learn where the unit's water shutoff is.
  • Report drips under sinks right away.
  • Run every fixture and flush every toilet between tenants to find leaks.
  • Leave heat on and follow freeze guidance when a unit sits empty over a break.

Permits and licensed work

Installing a new plumbing system or replacing water or waste piping in a Huntsville rental needs a City permit, and a licensed plumber handles the filing. Huntsville's adopted codes include the International Plumbing Code (IPC) 2024. Licensing rules in Texas generally call for a licensed plumber on this work. Ask for the license number and look it up with the state plumbing board before you agree to anything.

Before you call about a leak

  • Tell the property manager or owner right away.
  • Shut the unit's water valve if the leak is spreading.
  • Turn off breakers for any wet outlets.
  • Take dated photos of the damage.
  • Lift belongings off wet floors.
