Local knowledge

What Huntsville homes ask of their plumbing.

Chloraminated city water

The City of Huntsville's 2025 water quality report lists chloramines as the disinfectant, averaging 3.09 mg/L. The supply is a mix of city wells and surface water from the TRA Huntsville Regional system. The report gives no hardness figure, so test your tap before choosing a softener, and expect rubber seals and flappers to be a first wear point.

Permits and licensed work

City permit rules require a permit for new plumbing systems and for replacing water or waste piping. Inspections include pier, foundation and ground plumbing, top-out and final. A licensed plumber handles that paperwork, so your water heater or repipe job does not stall at the final inspection. The City of Huntsville lists the International Plumbing Code (IPC) 2024 among the codes it uses, and that is the reference a licensed plumber works from.

Sand, clay and roots

Walker County's forest soils are sands and clays, and the county averages roughly 46 inches of rain a year. Clay moves when it wets and dries, and pine and oak roots find leaking joints. That is why recurring clogs near the street side of a house often turn out to be a pipe problem and not a grease problem.

Campus rentals and downtown houses

Sam Houston State University sits near downtown Huntsville and I-45 serves the city. Rentals near campus and older homes around the courthouse square change hands often, so an unknown plumbing history is common. A camera look through a sewer line is a sensible first step before a purchase or a new lease.
