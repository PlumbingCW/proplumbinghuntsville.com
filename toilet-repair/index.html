Emergency Toilet Repair in Huntsville, TX

An overflowing toilet is a mess that gets worse by the minute. Lift the tank lid and push the flapper down, or turn the valve behind the toilet clockwise to stop the supply, and then call our 24/7 line in Huntsville. Do not flush again.

Toilet repair covers clogs, running water, leaks at the base and loose or cracked bowls. Most fixes are quick, and the plumber replaces only the part that failed, such as a flapper, fill valve, wax ring or flange, instead of the whole toilet.

What to do when a toilet will not stop

  • Lift the tank lid and push the flapper down.
  • Close the shutoff valve at the wall.
  • Do not flush again if the bowl is rising.
  • Towel the floor to protect the subfloor.
  • Switch off power to wet outlets.

Running toilet: the cheap fix and the expensive habit

A toilet that runs between flushes wastes water and raises your bill. The flapper that seals the tank is a rubber part, and rubber wears out. The City of Huntsville's water quality report lists chloramines as its disinfectant, and many plumbers see rubber parts age faster in chloraminated water, so a flapper replaced every few years is normal.

A fill valve that will not shut off, a chain that is too long or short and a cracked overflow tube cause the same symptom. Try the dye test, which is a few drops of food coloring in the tank. If color shows in the bowl without a flush, the flapper is leaking.

Leaks at the base and rocking toilets

Water around the base means the wax ring, the seal between the toilet and the drain flange, has failed or the flange is cracked or sits too low. Rocking toilets stress the ring and the floor under it. Left alone, the leak can rot the subfloor.

A plumber pulls the toilet, replaces the wax ring, inspects the flange and resets the toilet with new bolts. If the floor is soft, tell the dispatcher. Related fixture work, such as a stuck shutoff valve, is covered under faucet and valve repair.

Clogged toilets and when it is more than a clog

A plunger clears most single toilet clogs. If the bowl rises when you flush and then slowly drops, or if the tub gurgles when you flush, the blockage is past the toilet. That is a drain or sewer problem, and drain cleaning is the next step.

A closet auger reaches deeper than a plunger and can retrieve objects. Plumbers also check for a cracked toilet trap or a clog in the vent, which is why a fixture that clogs repeatedly deserves a visit.
