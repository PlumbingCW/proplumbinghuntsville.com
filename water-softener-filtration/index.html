Water Softener and Filtration in Huntsville, TX

Scale on fixtures, chalky glasses and a chlorine-like taste are all reasons to look at water treatment. In Huntsville we install and service water softeners and filters, but we recommend testing your tap first so the equipment matches what is actually in the water.

Water softeners and filtration systems treat household water. A softener removes the minerals that cause scale, while filters reduce things like chloramine taste, sediment and some contaminants. Which one you need depends on test results, not guesses.

Test first, buy second

The City of Huntsville's 2025 water quality report does not give a hardness figure, so test your own tap or ask the utility for its numbers. Hardness is measured in grains per gallon or mg/L, and a basic test strip or lab test gives the answer.

If your water is soft, a softener will not help. If it is hard, a softener protects water heaters, faucets and appliances from scale. If your complaint is taste, a carbon filter may be all you need.

Chloramine in Huntsville water

The City of Huntsville's 2025 report lists chloramines as the disinfectant, averaging 3.09 mg/L across the year with a range of 1.12 to 3.92 mg/L. Chloramine is a chlorine and ammonia compound used because it lasts longer in pipes. Ordinary carbon filters work on free chlorine more easily than on chloramine, so look for a filter rated for chloramine.

Chloramine can also wear rubber parts, which affects toilet flappers and some appliance seals. That is one reason a toilet repair often involves a flapper. We do not make health claims about water, so ask your physician about health questions and the city about the report.

Wells and private systems

Homes outside city water may use private wells, where water quality varies by location and well depth. A lab test is the right first step. Do not buy equipment before you know what is in the water. For work on supply lines tied to a treatment system, see plumbing installation.

Questions to ask before buying equipment

A salesperson who cannot tie the equipment to a test result is selling a product, not a solution.

  • Which test result is this equipment solving.
  • What are the ongoing costs for salt and filters.
  • Will the system need a drain and a power outlet nearby.
  • How is it serviced and by whom.
  • What happens if the unit fails.

Installation and service

  1. Test results are reviewed and the right equipment is chosen.
  2. A bypass valve and drain are installed and the unit is plumbed in.
  3. The unit is set to match your hardness and household size.
  4. The plumber explains salt refills or filter changes and when to service it.

Salt, brine and the drain

A salt softener flushes brine to a drain every few days. That discharge needs a proper air gap and a place to go. Homes on septic systems should ask the installer and the septic service provider whether the brine is acceptable. Potassium salts are an option for some, at a higher cost.
